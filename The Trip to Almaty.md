I am writing this, sitting at Almaty airport. The flight back to Delhi is there in one hour. 

Ryan and I are heading back, after 8 days stay in Almaty.

When any trip finishes, the usual question anyone ask and one also ponders is "How was it?".

Travel holds different meaning for different people. And the answer to "how was it" also depends on what you were looking for in the first place. 

Why I came to Almaty? I did almost zero research on Almaty from sightseeing perspective. My booking decision was influenced by great weather this time. Tanya has gone Almaty before and loved the city for walking. So someone you trust have visited the place and found it safe and lovely was an addition vote. 

And what I was looking for? For me, travel has evolved over the years. When I started to travel, the focus was to "visit"  as many famous places as possible. Sort of gathering proof that I'm a well travelled person. 

Then, over the years, it evolved to be closer to the nature. See beautiful places. One reason I love Europe so much, it is really beautiful. 

And now, my equation with travel is again changing. Now travel has less fixed agenda but more to see how the local lives. Observe the culture, day to day ongoings. Where they shop, eat, how they commute. To me, learning about a new place is educational and also creating new shared experiences when travelling with my kids. 

So, for Almaty, as I said, there was no sightseeing research. Only logistics work was done - Kazakhstan is visa free for India (and it was seamless to enter), so only flight and hotel was to be booked.



## Flight and reaching Almaty City

Almaty is 3 hours flight from Delhi. Smooth flight, no hiccups.  One thing I noticed that flight was full of students. I was aware Kazakhstan is famous for medical studies. I chatted with young girl sitting next to me. She was, as I guessed, studying medical and was in 4th year. sI asked her why not study in India, is Kazakh better. Her answered was not unexpected - In india seats are less, competition is so much more. And ofcourse the paper leaks! 

Immigration was seamless ; as no visa needed, the officer just looked into our passport and stamped in. Return ticket was not even asked.

Getting out, opened Yandex Go app (I had downloaded and configured this in India) and booked cab. Trip to city was 750 INR and took about 40"

For hotel I went with Novotel for first 4 days. Tanya had stayed in this area, so it was a safe bet to zero down. It was in market area, super location (Arbat Street). 



## Almaty Experience

Rather than writing a day by day blog (which is boring to write and read), I will capture some of my highlights from the trip

* City is designed to walk : The city is immensely walkable. The city is densely packed, roads are very clean, sidewalks and benches are everywhere.  Sidewalks makes walking safe and convenient. And benches are useful when you are tired or want to grab a meal on the go and want to sit down and eat. We ended up walking 20k steps almost every day.
* Parks Everywhere : Every km or so, we used to find a park. Well maintained, neat and clean. Not overly curated like some of the French gardens. But not unkempt either. We spent a lot of time in the parks - sometimes eating, sometime playing poker (I taught Ryan poker in this trip)
* Third Space : Home and office are the usual two spaces in people life. This city offers third space everywhere - parks with benches, market with benches. While there were lot of busy cafes and that culture is well alive, we noticed people were sitting on benches everywhere and chatting. This is something I miss deeply in India and it was wonderful to see it here. 
* Non touristy : This will sound strange, as I was a tourist (or traveller, as I like to call me) there. But this city doesnt have much to offer in terms of tourism. Famous spots inside main city can be covered in one day. There are many wonderful daylong trip outside city, but the main city itself is not very touristy. Which means, for someone like me who wanted to immerse in local culture, it was exactly the right city. 



## Day Trips from Almaty

* Calmyn Canyon : Booked a private taxi. It was 2.5 hours away from the city. Seeing the canyon which has been there for 10 million years was something. Had a good time there. 
* Ski resort shymbulak : took 3 levels of cable car ; extremely beautiful.
* Kuka fun park : Only good thing was fast track ride, which was scary and fun.
