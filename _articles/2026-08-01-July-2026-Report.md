This is the seventh edition of my monthly review. As I am writing this over months, I am realizing months and quarters have themes. 

If June was about presence with the kids in Japan, July was all about family. The house was full : family visited from Canada and Germany. And Riya counselling and college selection went on in parallel. One of the most heart fulfilling month this was!! 

---

July opened on Dad's birthday (1st July). He would have been 76. He left us 5 years ago. As time has gone by, while there is sadness, there is more feeling of gratitude. Mom and Papa gave us a good life, a good education, good values and I spent the morning reflecting back instead of grieving. That set an odd, fitting tone for a month that turned out to be almost entirely about family showing up for each other.

## Family & Relationships

For the first ten days of July, the house was as full as it gets : Mom, Riya, Ryan, Cherry and Aarvish, and Jatu with Suchi and the kids, all under one roof. "Most lovely feeling," I wrote on July 3 in my morning journal, "everyone making their own plans, in and out of the house." Cherry left for Germany mid-month, but Jatu and family were here entire July (going today - 10th August when I am writing this), and the warmth never really let up.

The centerpiece was a family road trip to Amritsar (July 12–14) : two Seltos, the whole crew, food and old-city walks. I took kids to GNDU, place where Tarun spent her childhood. It was lovely showing kids where their mother spent first 18 years of her life. 

Riya's year-long JEE and counselling marathon finally resolved this month : she got into **IIIT Delhi, Computer Science and Biotechnology**. A brilliant  outcome after two years of exams. She moved into the hostel on August 3rd, right on the border between this report and next month's.

Ryan and I kept building together (more in Learning below), took the kids to the arcade one evening ("super fun"), watched *UP* in the society theatre with the whole family, and swam together after. I also spent 3 business days in Pune (July 8–11)

The one real miss : **Bihar with Mom did not happen**, again. This time the honest reason is different from the last six months : Mom's own Canada travel date shifted because of the house work, so the "3rd week of July" hard gate moved out from under the plan before I even got to it. Streak intact, but for once not self-inflicted.

## Wellness & Fitness

17 gym sessions in a month with this much travel and family density is a decent number, and I'll take it. What didn't happen was the weight cut : the Q3 rock was 85kg to 80kg by August 4th, and it simply held flat. The honest cause is a genuinely food-heavy, social month, not neglect : Amritsar, birthdays, a full house eating together most nights :). It was just not the environment ; I should have seen and predicted batter. 

Rehab continued in the background, back-safe first per the L4/L5 diagnosis from June. No heroics, no re-stacking the load that caused the degradation in the first place.

The house work kept progressing : 

What didn't happen was the written deployment thesis I'd set as the July rock. Something that will become a key focus for me in August. 

## Learning & Writing

Two threads here, one quiet and one shared. Early in the month I started building what I'm calling a **Life OS** : a proper interface for my own planning system, evolving straight out of the daily journal and weekly plan templates I've been living in all year. "Coming up nice," I wrote on July 7, and there's something fitting about the planning system itself becoming a project worth building.

The shared thread was with Ryan and Jatu : we picked up Jatu's Maths app startup and worked to migrate to a different platform. Ryan, Jatu and I spent many days sitting on dining table, bouncing on the ideas together and building app. Whenever Ryan and I code together, it is so much fun. Grateful for this shared experience. 

On the more introspective side, a July 8th flight to Pune produced an honest bit of self-examination : a nagging feeling of being broadly competent but rarely deep in anything, of talking intelligently about fields but nagging feeling that I've not gone as deep as I can.  I named it half-jokingly as wanting to learn the Rubik's Cube under 60 seconds, but the real question underneath : where have I actually reached the top 5% of a field : is worth sitting with rather than resolving with another shiny new hobby. No article shipped in July. The publishing cadence, which held for most of H1, took a real pause this month.

## Mind & Meditation

The evening-journal one-line floor : the guardrail habit I installed after retiring the sit-meditation streak : mostly didn't land. Most days in July, that field sat empty. What did hold, every single day without exception, was the morning affirmation : *I am a warrior monk*. Small, but it's the one mind-pillar habit that's actually sticking, and worth noticing rather than dismissing just because it's not the bigger goal.

## Joy

July's joy was almost entirely relational : the full house, Amritsar, friend's birthday, the arcade with the kids, movie night, and building the Maths app with Ryan and Jatu. No scheduled novelty, no micro-adventure : that's retired, and I'm not missing it. The pattern from every month this year holds again: joy comes from people and building, not from manufacturing new experiences.

## Travel

Amritsar with the extended family and  few days in Pune were both real, good trips. Bihar with Mom is the seventh-month carry now, and for the first time the reason is genuinely external : her own travel calendar moved, not mine.

## Office

Kept steady, as always in these posts. Nothing dramatic to report either way this month : worth a closer look in August so I'm not guessing again next time I write this.

## Looking Ahead to August

August opens with real milestones already behind it : Riya settling into IIIT Delhi, my 48th birthday surrounded by family, and the house work accelerating faster than expected. The center of gravity for the month is a physio-cleared return to real training (careful, not a race back to where June left off), turning Boundless from research into an actual written thesis, and rebooking Bihar the moment Mom's new travel date is real. The evening-journal habit needs an actual trigger this time, not just intention repeated for the fourth month running.

Life keeps proving the same thing this year: the things I schedule with a hard external gate get done, and the quiet solo ones don't, unless I build a real trigger around them too.

*I am a warrior monk.*
