This is the ninth edition of my monthly review. September was the last month of Q3, so like June's edition, this one contains two reviews : a look at how September went, and an honest look back at the whole quarter. And just like that, 75% of the year is gone. 

---

September was built around two birthdays and two trips. **Ryan turned 13 on the 3rd. Riya turned 18 on the 20th.** What a milestone month : one child became a teenager and the other became an adult. I did a trip with each of them : Almaty with Ryan, Krabi with Riya. In the middle of it all was the fifth anniversary of losing Papa.

September was also my best training month of the year. **In August I did zero cardio sessions. In September I did ten.** I'll explain below what I think made the difference.

Looking back at the whole quarter, Q3 had three clear chapters. July was the house full : family visiting from Canada, a food trip to Amritsar, long dinners together. August was the house emptying : Riya to her college hostel, Jatu and then Mom flying back to Canada, and a long chapter of family history closing. September was about milestones. It was a full, warm quarter, centred on people. 

## Family & Relationships

For the ninth month running, this was the strongest pillar.

**Ryan turned 13.** I started his day by making him pancakes for breakfast, which he loves. Then we cracked open a new murder mystery and took two hours to solve it, went to the arcade (we go every six to eight weeks, and we both love it), and grabbed a pizza on the way back. He is such a kind boy. Let's see what the teenage years do to him.

A week later, on 11th Sept, we flew to **Almaty** for a week. There was no fixed agenda, and that was the point. We rode the alpine ride at Kok Tobe twice and walked 3.5km back to the hotel afterwards (I was surprised by how much stronger he's getting). We stood inside a ten-million-year-old canyon at Charyn (yes, 10 million years old), took three cable cars up to 3,200m at Shymbulak, rode e-scooters around the city, and played poker on a park bench between feeding the pigeons. From my journal:

> Happy to have slow lovely days with my boy.

**Riya turned 18.** The evening before, we went to the gym together. At midnight we cut a tiramisu cake and called family, and on the day itself we went bowling and had pizza. My baby is an adult now.

At the end of the month we flew to **Krabi**, just the two of us. We took a boat to Hong Island, spent evenings at the Ao Nang market, and had long afternoons by the pool, both of us reading. She was immersed in her own Bloodlines novel, the same series I had just finished. I loved that.

**The 25th was five years since we lost Papa.** I chose to celebrate the day rather than only grieve it.

> I miss him dearly and I talk to him regularly. But I want to remember him smiling and be happy. Mumma, Jatu and I are still very close unit and this was the foundation laid by Mom Dad.

On 25th, I cooked mutton and palak dal for my friend, and it turned into a leisurely four-hour lunch. In the evening, as I was heading to Bangla Sahib, Ryan asked if he could come along. My boy is so full of love. Bangla Sahab is my annual tradition on this day ; Papa used to love this place. 

Friends filled the rest of the month: long lunches, a movie with a friend, and a whole Sunday spent going from café to café with another friend. 

## Wellness & Fitness

I'm quite proud of this month, especially after August.

In August I planned twelve cardio sessions and did zero. For September I planned six. When I looked at the data at the end of the month:

- **Swim 5, bike 2, run 3 : ten cardio sessions**, plus **seven strength sessions**
- **359,000 steps** : an average of 11,977 a day, with 17 days over 10k and 5 days over 20k

And this was without a coach. Two things helped. My watch and phone tracked everything, so I could see where I stood. And training was on my morning "Today's 3" list on 11 of the 17 days I filled it in. More on that below.

The scale was the other story. After Almaty I was **88kg**, which was a shocker and exactly the wake-up call I needed. I made a daily meal plan, tracked it, and was at 85.2 within a week. Then Krabi happened, and this morning I'm 87.2 again. Both trips added weight. So I need to make eating right on the road easier : carry a portable blender, protein, ground seeds and nuts, and creatine, or just have curd, which is available almost anywhere. And carry resistance bands for days without a gym. Reduce the friction and compliance will follow.

Sleep averaged six to six and a half hours, and I slept well in both Almaty and Krabi. One morning after a good night, I wrote: *"Sleep has such direct correlation with energy."*

Across the quarter, July was rehab for my back (the cost, as I wrote in June, of long hours at a desk and no mobility work), August was zero, and September was the best training month of the year. The Q3 rock was to rebuild the body and cut weight. Training is finally back on track. Weight isn't, yet.

No standalone article this month. For now, these reviews are my writing.

## Mind & Meditation

The weekly plan came back this month, after disappearing for two weeks in August. September showed how much it matters. As I wrote in my notes: *what gets planned in the weekly gets executed.*

The Sunday reflection, my replacement for the evening journal, didn't happen even once. In August's post I promised that if it failed too, I'd delete the field from the template and stop pretending. So that's what I'm doing. The weekly review already does that job.

Something else I noticed. Solitude kept showing up in my gratitude notes : *"the freedom and joy of solitude," "grateful for being happy in my own company."* At the start of the year, loneliness was one of the fears I wrote down. Now I'm actually enjoying my own company.

One last honest note. Sitting by the pool in Krabi with no agenda, I noticed how much of my attention goes to shallow entertainment, mostly Instagram, which in my own words *"doesn't even feel good."* It's coming off my phone for October.

## What Actually Gets Done

This is the most useful thing September taught me, so it gets its own section.

Every morning's journal entry starts with a "Today's 3" : three things worth a chunk of my finite time that day. I filled it in on 17 mornings in September. Here's what went into it:

| Area | Mornings in "Today's 3" | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Training (swim, gym, steps, physio) | 11 of 17 | Beat the plan: 10 cardio + 7 strength |
| Work email | 11 of 17 | Under control |
| Time with Ryan | 3 | His birthday, a movie, an afternoon together |
| French | 1 | Stopped after a week |
| Investing | 0 | Nothing moved |

For two quarters I've been telling myself that *solo* goals fail and *social* goals succeed, because other people create deadlines. September doesn't fit that story. My training was entirely solo, and it worked because it was on the list most mornings. Investing wasn't on the list once.

So it isn't really about solo vs social. **It's about whether something gets a slot in my day.** If it's on my morning list, it gets done. If it's not, it doesn't happen, however important I think it is.

And the uncomfortable bit : work email took one of my three slots on 11 mornings. Finance, which I keep saying matters most for the next phase of my life, got none.

## Looking Ahead

I'm 48, and I'll turn 50 in two years. Sitting by the pool in Krabi with nowhere to be, I wrote down what I want the next phase of my life to be about. In Hindu tradition, life is divided into four *ashramas* of roughly 25 years each. I'm not planning to give up worldly life, but I am thinking hard about the next 25 years.

Two goals came out of it:

- **Financial independence.** Get my money structured properly, so it can grow and support the life I want.
- **Endurance, done properly.** I have ten years of endurance sport behind me, and I believe I'm far from my potential. The dream : a **sub-10 Ironman**, built on strength training and good nutrition.

So Q4 has three rocks:

1. **Lay the foundation for financial independence.** Work with a fee-only adviser, come out with a written plan and a real number, and then put the idle money to work against that plan. My own stock research comes after the plan, not before it.
2. **Rebuild the endurance base.** Train every week, including travel weeks, with a minimum to hold on the road. Mobility every day, which is the real lesson of June's back injury. And 80kg by mid-December.
3. **Canada in December.** Riya, Ryan and I spend the holidays with Mom and Jatu, and bring Mom home with us. It needs a fair amount of paperwork in October.

For October specifically: the trip paperwork, the first conversation with an adviser, my full training split at home with a minimum in Goa, French on a fixed number of days a week, and Instagram off the phone. Bihar stays off for 2026, but I'll put it on the 2027 calendar as soon as Mom's return flight is booked.

The goals aren't what's different this time. **What's different is that each one gets a morning.**

*I am a warrior monk.*
